LANGUAGE REVIEW OF THE FIRST DRAFTS
Targets: 01_draft/pitch.txt, 01_draft/report.txt
Goal: sound like an engineer wrote it, read as one continuous argument, and
survive a reviewer who reads both documents back to back.


A. REPEATED CONSTRUCTIONS (counted, not guessed)

  "rather than"                10x   <- the worst offender
  "which is"                    7x
  "the same"                    7x
  "follow(s)"                   5x
  "Two costs follow"            2x   <- verbatim in BOTH documents
  "no new material..."          2x   <- verbatim in BOTH documents

"rather than" is a hedge that lets a sentence assert and disclaim at once.
Ten of them in five pages reads as a machine balancing every clause. Keep at
most two per document, in the places where the contrast is the actual point
(the fold vs. approaching it; infeasible vs. costly). Everywhere else, cut the
disclaimer and let the sentence commit.


B. STRUCTURAL TICS

1. Announce-then-enumerate. "Two costs follow." / "The first is voltage." /
   "The second is travel." Used identically in both documents. A reviewer
   reading the pitch then the report sees the same scaffolding twice. Keep it
   once, in the report, where a numbered structure is expected. In the pitch,
   let the two costs arrive as consequences inside running prose.

2. Negate-then-assert. "Pull-in is not the maximum of a smooth curve. It is a
   saddle-node bifurcation." This is the single most recognisable LLM cadence.
   Once is fine and it is genuinely the right move here, because the negation
   carries real information. It appears in both documents. Keep it in the
   report only.

3. Tricolons. "silicon area, quiescent power, and one more block that can
   fail." / "One reverse pass, sixty nodes, no finite differences and no
   fitted model in between." Three-item lists land well once a page and turn
   into wallpaper at three a page. Break at least two into a pair or a four.

4. Signposting. "Two entries carry most of the weight." / "One detail earns
   its place." / "Three things are worth stating plainly." These tell the
   reader what is coming instead of just delivering it. Delete the announcement
   and keep the content; the emphasis survives.

5. Summary sentences that restate the previous sentence. "This inverts the
   standard pipeline." adds nothing after the sentence before it. Cut.

6. Mechanical parallelism. The three capabilities in the pitch are three
   one-sentence paragraphs of near-identical length and shape. Vary them:
   the amortized network deserves two sentences, the RL policy one, inverse
   design a half-line.


C. FLOW AND CONNECTION

1. The pitch's NOVELTY section opens by re-asserting what THE AI APPROACH just
   established ("Nobody differentiates through the MEMS pull-in bifurcation").
   The reader has just been told. Novelty should advance the argument: what is
   new is the combination, so open on what exists nearby and where it stops.

2. Pitch PROBLEM ends on the surrogate handicap; AI APPROACH opens on making
   physics differentiable. That join works. Preserve it, and build the same
   kind of join everywhere else: end each section on the thing the next one
   answers.

3. The report's Section 1 currently paraphrases the pitch's PROBLEM almost
   sentence for sentence. They are read by the same people. The pitch should
   argue from consequence (cost, yield, area); the report should state the
   mechanism once, tersely, and move on. No shared sentences.

4. The report has no bridge into Section 3. "Datasets" arrives cold after the
   methodology. One clause tying it back ("the gradient just described is what
   removes the need for a dataset") makes the section order feel intentional
   instead of imposed by the call for papers.

5. Figure references in the report draft are inconsistent with the actual
   files. Mapping to use: Fig0=architecture, Fig1=inverse design and exponent
   reversal, Fig2=RL and method comparison, Fig4=validation. In LaTeX use
   \ref so the numbering cannot drift.

E. WHAT IS ALREADY WORKING, DO NOT SAND IT OFF

  - Admitting the strawman surrogate and what the solver actually returned
    (-639458). Specific failure numbers read as lab notes, not marketing.
  - "the surrogate baselines we built to compete against ourselves".
  - The cube-law check described as the test that would have caught a
    plausible but wrong adjoint. That sentence is what a reviewer needs.
  - Naming Haluzan's conjecture and then answering it with two numbers.

  These are the parts that sound like a person did the work. The rewrite must
  not smooth them into uniform prose.


F. ONE CONTENT GAP

The pitch never says what the reader should do with the result. It ends on
"everything is released". Close on the reader instead: this is a licence-free
benchmark anyone in the room can run on a laptop this afternoon.
