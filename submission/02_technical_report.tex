\title{Cutting MEMS Drive Voltage by 41\%:\\
Inverse Design Through the Pull-In Bifurcation}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Tushar Dudeja}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Electrostatic MEMS actuators are held back by two limits that share one cause:
drive voltages too high for a CMOS supply, and only a third of the gap usable
before pull-in destroys the device. Pull-in is a saddle-node bifurcation, where
the tangent stiffness is singular and ordinary differentiable solvers fail. We
solve the \emph{extended fold system} directly and obtain exact gradients of the
pull-in voltage with respect to electrode geometry via the implicit function
theorem. Three data-free capabilities follow: gradient-based inverse design,
which cuts drive voltage $22.86\rightarrow13.60$\,V ($-41\%$) by reshaping the
gap alone, with no new process step or mask; an amortized design network trained
end-to-end through the physics with \emph{zero} stored simulations, emitting a
specification-satisfying geometry in $0.04$\,ms; and a reinforcement-learning
policy that infers each device's unmeasurable fabrication ceiling, recovering
$+12.8\%$ usable travel with $0.0\%$ of devices destroyed. The solver is
validated against eight independent sources spanning 1967--2025, including a
reproduced \emph{reversal} of the optimal gap exponent between cantilever and
fixed--fixed geometries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}

Electrostatic actuation dominates commercial MEMS --- micromirror arrays, RF
switches, tunable capacitors, optical attenuators --- and runs into two hard
limits that are the same instability. First, a device needing tens of volts
cannot be driven from the logic supply, so it carries an on-chip charge pump
that costs die area, power and an additional reliability failure mode. Second,
stable travel ends at one third of the gap; beyond it the structure snaps onto
the electrode. Because the exact snap threshold moves with fabrication
tolerance and cannot be measured on a device \emph{without destroying it},
drive schedules must be set for the worst-case die, and every good die on the
wafer is then under-driven.

Design practice attacks this with FEM campaigns over hand-picked geometry
families, in licensed tools that expose no gradients. Current AI practice
inherits the same limitation: fit a neural surrogate to $\sim$10$^4$
simulations, then optimise through the surrogate's approximation error. We
remove the intermediary (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Governing equations}
A beam of plate modulus $\tilde{E}$ and thickness $t$ over a shaped electrode
gap $d(x)$ obeys, in nondimensional form ($x=l\xi$, $y=d_0Y$, $G=D-Y$),
\begin{equation}
Y'''' - N_t\,Y'' \;=\; \Lambda\,\frac{1+\alpha G}{G^{2}},
\label{eq:beam}
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda = 6\varepsilon_0V^2l^4/(\tilde{E}t^3d_0^3)$ the normalized load,
$N_t$ residual-stress tension and $\alpha$ a fringing-field correction.
Fourth-order finite differences turn \eqref{eq:beam} into a residual
$R(Y,\Lambda)$.

\subsection{Differentiating through the fold}
Pull-in is not a maximum of a smooth function but a \emph{turning point}: the
tangent stiffness $J=\partial R/\partial Y$ becomes singular, so neither a
forward solve nor its derivative survives there. We instead solve the extended
system of Keller and Seydel,
\begin{equation}
R(Y,\Lambda)=0,\qquad J(Y,\Lambda)\,v=0,\qquad v^{\!\top}v-1=0,
\label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
for $(Y,\Lambda,v)$ in a single Newton solve, warm-started from a coarse
displacement-controlled sweep. The bifurcation is thus a \emph{root} of a
regular system rather than a singularity to be approached, and the implicit
function theorem gives $\partial\Lambda_{\rm PI}/\partial d(x)$ exactly --- not
a finite difference, not a surrogate estimate.

Globalization is what makes this usable: an Armijo line search with a
fraction-to-the-boundary rule, and row equilibration in the
displacement-controlled stage. Without the latter, an $O(1)$ constraint row
against $O(10^6)$ beam rows prevented convergence on shaped profiles. The gap
floor is $10^{-12}$, not $10^{-6}$, so a penetrating state produces an enormous
residual and is rejected rather than converging onto a nonphysical branch.

\section{Datasets}

\textbf{This work uses no training dataset}, and that is the methodological
point rather than an omission. Every gradient that trains every network here
comes from a live solve of \eqref{eq:fold}; the amortized network of
Sec.~\ref{sec:arch} consumed \textbf{6{,}000 live solves and 0 stored samples}.

Two categories of data do appear, and we separate them explicitly. (i) The
surrogate \emph{baselines} we built for comparison are the only components that
consume sampled data: 400 solves each, generated on demand by the included
scripts under active learning. (ii) Validation targets are numerical values
transcribed from the literature --- geometry, material properties, measured
pull-in voltages --- each cited at its point of use in the source file.
Third-party PDFs are not redistributed; the index in \texttt{papers/README.md}
gives full citations and DOIs.

\section{Model architecture}\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Amortized design network}
The design is parameterized as $d(\xi)=\mathrm{softplus}(\sum_{k<K}
p_k\cos k\pi\xi)$ with $K=12$ cosine modes on $N=40$ nodes, together with a
scalar gap scale $\sigma$. A network maps the specification (required travel
$T$) to that geometry: inputs $[(T-T_{\rm lo})/(T_{\rm hi}-T_{\rm lo}),\log T]$,
a $2\!\to\!64\!\to\!64$ $\tanh$ trunk, and two linear heads --- a shape head to
the $K$ coefficients and a scale head to $\log\sigma$.

\subsection{The hard-constraint enforcement layer}
A soft penalty on the travel constraint is \emph{structurally} biased here,
because reducing travel also reduces $V_{\rm PI}$: the optimizer is rewarded for
violating the specification, and settles at $\sim$5\% error. We replace it with
a differentiable enforcement layer in the spirit of OptNet and HardNet. Four
Newton steps solve $\mathrm{travel}(p,\sigma)=T$ for $\sigma$, followed by one
correction step whose $p$-dependence is kept live while
$\partial\,\mathrm{travel}/\partial\sigma$ is frozen. At convergence this
reproduces $d\sigma^\ast/dp=-F_p/F_\sigma$ --- the same implicit-function
argument used for \eqref{eq:fold} itself --- so the constraint holds \emph{and}
the shape head still receives correct gradients. The loss carries no penalty
term at all, and the scale head is regressed onto
$\mathrm{stopgrad}(\sigma^\ast)$ so that inference remains a single forward
pass. Specification error falls from $\sim$5\% to $0.56\%$ mean over
$T=1$--$3\,\mu$m.

\subsection{Baselines and control policy}
The strongest surrogate baseline is a 1D Fourier Neural Operator (width 32, 12
retained modes, depth 4), given active learning, a trust region and feasible
restarts so that it is not a strawman; an MLP surrogate is included for
reference. The control policy is a $7\!\to\!64\!\to\!1$ $\tanh$ network trained
by REINFORCE with a batch-mean baseline and Gaussian exploration
($\sigma=0.10$). Pathwise gradients are inapplicable because episode
termination is hard and irreversible.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation}
\input{../figures/tables.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:validation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:valid} summarize agreement with
eight independent sources, 1967--2025. Two results are load-bearing. The exact
gradient reproduces the analytic $c^3$ scaling law to $0.00\%$, confirming that
differentiation through \eqref{eq:fold} is correct rather than merely
plausible. And the \emph{optimal gap exponent reverses} between geometries
(Fig.~\ref{fig:design}(b),(c)): $n^*\!=\!1.29$ for cantilevers against $4/3$
reported by Haluzan \emph{et al.}, and $n^*\!=\!1.00$ for fixed--fixed beams,
matching their monotone trend. Opposite trends in two geometries are not
obtainable by fitting. On the discriminating case of Seeger and Boser, two
designs that are mechanically identical and differ only in parasitic
capacitance are correctly predicted to fail by \emph{different} mechanisms.

\subsection{Inverse design: the 41\%}
Minimizing $V_{\rm PI}$ at fixed stable travel by gradient descent on a
free-form gap profile, with the scale set by the enforcement layer so that
every iterate is feasible, takes the cantilever benchmark from $22.86$\,V to
$13.60$\,V --- a $41\%$ reduction obtained \emph{from electrode geometry
alone}, with no new material, process step or mask. Starting from a
\emph{uniform} gap and given no knowledge of any prior design family, the
optimizer rediscovers the hand-tuned profile of Haluzan \emph{et al.}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:design}(a)) and descends $22.9\rightarrow13.9$\,V unaided, which
rules out a favourable initialization. Table~\ref{tab:inverse} reports both
boundary conditions.

This also settles an open question. Haluzan \emph{et al.} conjectured that gains
beyond simple gap shapes ``are believed to be minimal'' but could not test it.
Free-form optimization over the entire profile measures what remains:
$0.14\%$ (cantilever) and $1.45\%$ (fixed--fixed).

\subsection{Against surrogate modeling, at equal budget}
At an \emph{equal} budget of 400 true solves, an MLP surrogate reaches
$17.20$\,V and the FNO $14.25$\,V, against $13.78$\,V for direct
differentiation and $13.64$\,V for the amortized network
(Table~\ref{tab:methods}). We report plainly that the FNO is competitive: our
advantage is that we need no training data, no architecture search and
$40\times$ less wall-clock, not superior accuracy.

\subsection{Recovering travel lost to tolerance}
The tip-in ceiling $r_{\rm ti}=1/(1+k_xL_c^2/6k_\theta)$ varies device to device
and is not measurable at test time, so fixed-gain control must target the worst
case, forfeiting $19\%$ of travel. A policy observing only the device's own
tilt response recovers $80.4\pm4.9\%$ of that range --- $+12.8\%$ usable travel
--- with $0.0\pm0.0\%$ of devices destroyed over $1{,}536$ held-out episodes.
That it genuinely \emph{adapts}, rather than being uniformly less conservative,
is shown by the correlation between achieved travel and each device's own
ceiling: $\rho=+0.96$ against $-0.03$ for fixed gain (Fig.~\ref{fig:ai}(a)).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

Absolute voltages carry a systematic offset: $+5.4\%$ against the M-TEST
measurement, and $\sim$$+85\%$ for the 2025 microbridge, where matching would
require an effective length of $162\,\mu$m against a drawn $120\,\mu$m. The
evidence points to anchor compliance, absent from our ideal-clamp 1D model;
relative and trend predictions agree within 2.8\,pp, which is why the $41\%$
above is stated as a \emph{relative} claim on one process. The static solver
requires float64 (fp32 does not converge, $|R|=2.0$ versus $10^{-8}$) and is
therefore CPU-bound on consumer GPUs, which run fp64 at $\sim$1/64 rate. The
amortized network \emph{matches} rather than beats converged optimization
($13.636$ vs $13.665$\,V, inside the $0.28\%$ spread across restarts).
Fixed--fixed profiles with $n\gtrsim1.8$ are infeasible rather than merely
costly --- travel saturates below specification at any scale --- and are
excluded rather than plotted.
